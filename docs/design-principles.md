# Design principles

This page states the rules that every screen of the app follows.
Tokens, sizes and per-surface details live in [design-language.md](design-language.md).

## One thing at a time

A screen asks one question.
Everything else waits folded until the reader opens it.

Setup walks four steps, each opening on the question it answers.
The rest folds behind the step, closed on every launch.
Within the encode step's folded rows, opening one closes the one open before it.
The settings dialog shows one section.
Outside the grid, the viewer puts one stream on the stage.
The stats panel opens with every block closed, each heading carrying one figure.

## What needs the reader stands out

Healthy things stay quiet, so the one thing that needs attention reads first.

Red marks a check that blocks, a failure sentence and this computer sharing.
It stays scarce.
Amber marks a stream that runs but disappoints.
Green marks the way in.
Over video, weight carries emphasis.
Hues stay off the picture.
The reason a control is inert reads before anything else on it.

## Plain words

Copy reads like a colleague talking: short sentences, everyday words, the app's own terms.
A stream, a viewer, sharing, this computer.
An error names the problem, its cause and the fix.

## Show what holds

A control shows its state, so nothing asks the reader to remember the last click.
A menu row names a state and carries a tick while the backend reports it holds.

## Never invent a figure

A figure that nothing measured prints `…` and never zero.
A greyed control says why.
A setting the app cannot honour gets refused with a reason (no substitute behind the reader's back).

## One owner per value

A control appears in one window.
A window showing it elsewhere shows it read-only, with one link to the owner.

## Every press answers

A button fills on hover and darkens under the press.
A control waiting on the backend shows a turning arc in place of its label until the answer lands.

## Everything moves

Every change on screen eases into place, so the app feels fluent under the hand.
A control shown, hidden, picked or pressed fades, grows or slides.
A stream taking the stage fades in.
Small feedback answers each touch: a fill easing in, a give under the press, a chevron leaning the way it acts.
A control that appears on hover fades in softly, rounded where it sits on a picture.
Durations and easings: [design-language.md](design-language.md), "Motion".

## An error leaves the app

Every error text can be selected and copied.
It wraps and never ends in an ellipsis, since the reader came for its tail.

## Narrow windows

A tiling desktop can hand a window a quarter of the screen.
Below 520 px a panel rises as a sheet from the foot.
Most buttons keep their icon alone there.
The strip commit takes its short word.
